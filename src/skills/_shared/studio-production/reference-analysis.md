# Reference Video Director

Use this when a supplied video or URL is inspiration rather than raw footage to
edit. Analyze what makes it work, then create a new piece; do not copy its
surface styling or claims.

## Analysis Contract

1. Inspect the source with `analyze_video`. Transcribe speech when it affects
   structure or timing.
2. Record five dimensions in the brief or proposal:
   - content promise and audience;
   - hook and narrative structure;
   - pacing, shot duration, and transition rhythm;
   - visual system, typography, framing, and asset types;
   - audio architecture: voice, music, silence, and effects.
3. Separate `keep` from `change`. Keep transferable principles such as pacing
   or reveal order. Change topic-specific imagery, words, branding, and the
   piece's visual identity.
4. Produce at least two concepts with different creative mechanisms, not two
   palettes applied to the same layout.
5. If matching the reference would require unavailable tools, state the gap in
   the proposal and choose an honest alternative before asset generation.

## Sample-First Rule

For expensive or identity-sensitive work, the proposal should recommend a
10-15 second sample containing the hook and one representative middle beat.
The sample is evidence about style, pacing, and tool fit, not a miniature final
video with every idea compressed into it.

## Failure Conditions

- generic praise without timestamps or observable evidence;
- copying the reference's wording, logos, characters, or exact scene design;
- promising live-action motion while planning only still-image parallax;
- describing a tool or provider that Makaron cannot actually call.
