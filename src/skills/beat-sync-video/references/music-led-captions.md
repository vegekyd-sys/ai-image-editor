# Music-Led Captions

Give text a point of view and a rhythmic job. Write natural short phrases
in the language of the brief. An opening promise can build curiosity, a verb
can name the change at the instant it happens, and the result can answer the
opening. Use recurring cues across the story, with room for shots that already
speak clearly. A title and an end card alone leave the active section without
caption direction.

Compose each text cue around the footage and exact musical phrase. Decide
what the viewer should read, which word or part of the phrase should land,
and how it should arrive, hold, and leave. An offbeat can introduce a word
that resolves on a kick. Part of a phrase can appear with an action, then
change weight or position on the next accent. A reveal can stay completely
still so a detail lands cleanly. These are possible treatments for local
moments. Choose the type, movement, screen position, and density that belong
to this story. Vary the response across phrases where the audio or action
changes, and retain a coherent voice within the video.

- Put editorial text entrances, emphasis, and exits on the same measured
  timeline as the cut. Pick a specific beat or action for the meaningful
  change. Give the readable state enough time at phone size and protect the
  subject, hands, and platform controls.
- For spoken captions, follow `skills/_shared/spoken-caption.md` and
  `skills/_shared/speech-clock.md`. Measured speech owns words and cue
  boundaries. Music can accent the styling while that cue is visible. On a
  music-led edit without speech, write editorial captions grounded in the
  footage.
- For TikTok/Douyin, use the safe layout and editorial direction in
  `skills/tiktok-video/SKILL.md` and its platform layout reference. Build
  text in the editable Composition with a stable timeline and one visible text
  host per cue. Choose any creative variation during direction and preserve
  the result across preview and export.
- Review representative text hits at the opening, middle, and back half.
  Check the exact animated state and a stable reading frame, then watch how
  neighboring cues flow at normal speed. Fix repeated motion that feels
  mechanical, overlapping phrases, and effects that steal the action.
