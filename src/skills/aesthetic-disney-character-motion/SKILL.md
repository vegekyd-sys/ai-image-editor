# Character Motion Lens

Adapted from Dylan Tarre's MIT-licensed Animation Principles skills. Apply it
only through `skills/_shared/aesthetic-lens-contract.md`.

## Select, Do Not Sprinkle

Choose the three or four animation principles that best express this subject.
Do not mechanically apply all twelve to every scene.

- **Staging**: one unmistakable read; clear the stage before a new idea.
- **Anticipation**: a short reverse pose or light build before decisive action.
- **Squash and stretch**: preserve volume and identity; use for living/elastic
  forms, not logos, screenshots, or every piece of text.
- **Arcs**: organic subjects travel in curves; mechanical subjects may use
  lines intentionally.
- **Follow-through and overlap**: hat, limbs, glow, shadow, particles, or nearby
  props settle after the primary body.
- **Secondary action**: small expressive behavior supports, never competes.
- **Timing and exaggeration**: calibrate weight, comedy, and genre.
- **Solid spatial drawing**: maintain volume, perspective, light direction, and
  recognizable silhouette across poses.
- **Appeal**: silhouette, expression, and motion should invite another look.

## Mascot Performance

When the reference is a mascot or character, treat it as a performer rather
than a sticker pasted on scenes:

- establish a readable neutral pose before transformation
- give each scene one intention and one pose change
- let eyes/face/core light lead attention
- preserve defining silhouette and costume through every derivative asset
- stage size boldly enough that expression is readable
- use reaction and recovery poses to connect scenes

For non-character subjects, reinterpret the same principles through camera,
type, diagrams, light, and material without inventing a cartoon persona.

## Handoff

Record the selected principles and the hero/anticipation/recovery poses in the
storyboard. Composition should use deterministic frame poses and interpolated
in-betweens; no CSS transitions or unsupported animation runtime is required.
