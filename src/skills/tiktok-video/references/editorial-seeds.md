# Editorial seeds for a source-led cut

Use the selected prompts in Concept. Point to the first visible frame, a cut
or hold, and the last visible event. Follow an explicit brief or an accepted
edit on revision. If footage cannot support a choice, swap that axis and
identify the missing shot. The codes stay in planning. For several variants
in one run, change at least two axes between siblings. Keep the Skill's sound
contract.

## A. What is the viewer watching for?

1. **Prediction.** Stop just before contact. What does the viewer expect to happen?
2. **Witness.** Stay with the person or object receiving the action. What changes for them?
3. **Detective.** Show a trace of the outcome. Which earlier detail explains it?
4. **Scale.** Start on something too close to identify. When does the whole become legible?
5. **Test.** Set one visible criterion. Can the source show it being met or missed?
6. **Collector.** Find a recurring gesture or shape in separate moments. What breaks the pattern?
7. **Craft eye.** What adjustment would a first-time viewer overlook? Give it room to register.
8. **Counterpoint.** Find two moments that suggest opposing readings of the same subject.

## B. How does the footage move?

1. **Continuous witness.** Play one unbroken passage at normal speed. Let the change happen inside that shot; choose the passage for its ending.
2. **Two times in one frame.** Put two non-overlapping source moments in adjacent windows for the main beat. Align a landmark so both moments can be compared before returning to full frame.
3. **Reverse investigation.** Start on the consequence. Move back through at least two earlier clues in reverse order, each excerpt playing forward.
4. **Contact sheet in motion.** Build a short sequence of held source frames on screen together. Let exactly one move to reveal what changed.
5. **Replay with a second look.** Play one action twice. Give the first pass context, then use an enlarged crop and a slower hold on the overlooked instant of the same action.
6. **Shape relay.** Make hard match cuts across non-adjacent instants with a contour in the same screen location. Choose and name the contour before choosing shots.
7. **Stop and inspect.** Freeze the opening just before a change. Isolate a detail from that very frame, release back into the same moving passage, and finish there.
8. **Off-screen reveal.** Begin on a tight moving crop that hides the subject. Only the ending opens the frame enough to identify it.

## C. What earns the last frame?

1. **Bookend.** Revisit the opening image after its meaning has changed.
2. **Aftermath.** Hold on the first visible consequence; end after the viewer can identify it.
3. **Loop.** Cut on a motion or shape that the opening frame can actually complete on replay.
4. **Coda.** End with the exception, unanswered gesture or residual detail that complicates the pattern.

During Review, inspect the cut without graphic overlays. If the core operation
vanished into a chronological highlight reel, rebuild that passage before delivery.
