## A. What is the viewer watching for?

1. **Prediction.** Stop just before contact. What does the viewer expect to happen?
2. **Witness.** Stay with the person or object receiving the action. What changes for them?
3. **Detective.** Show a trace of the outcome. Which earlier detail explains it?
4. **Scale.** Start on something too close to identify. When does the whole become legible?
5. **Test.** Set one visible criterion. Can the source show it being met or missed?
6. **Collector.** Find a recurring gesture or shape in separate moments. What breaks the pattern?
7. **Craft eye.** What adjustment would a first-time viewer overlook? Give it room to register.
8. **Counterpoint.** Find two moments that suggest opposing readings of the same subject.

## B. How does the footage move?

1. Hold one continuous take through the decisive action. Cut away only after it lands.
2. Alternate two source moments. Return to the same framing so the change is easy to see.
3. Walk backward through causes using forward-playing excerpts. Preserve the truth of the action.
4. Give three related gestures progressively shorter beats, then let the last one breathe.
5. Play a passage once at context scale. Revisit just the telling instant at detail scale.
6. Match movement, contour or gaze across cuts. Let the match lead the viewer between times.
7. Interrupt motion with one held evidence frame. Release directly back into the moving shot.
8. Delay the wide view. Follow a detail across time before showing where it belongs.

## C. What earns the last frame?

1. Revisit the opening image after its meaning has changed.
2. Hold on the first visible consequence; end after the viewer can identify it.
3. Cut on a motion or shape that the opening frame can actually complete on replay.
4. End with the exception, unanswered gesture or residual detail that complicates the pattern.

In Concept, describe the first visible frame, one identifiable cut or hold, and
the final visible event. For multiple variants in one run, vary at least two
axes across siblings. If two ideas still yield the same opening and shot order
when stripped of text and music, recut one. During Review, locate those three
moments in the rendered frames before accepting the direction.
